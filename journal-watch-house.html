House history · 04 May 2026 · 4 min read

The watch room window

The broad window at the top of the old house was built for a practical purpose: to keep watch on the water. It still faces the same bearing today.

In 1962, the coastguard station opened with a clear view across the western approach. The keepers logged passing vessels by hand, noting their names, direction, and time. On storm nights, the window was the closest thing the station had to a front line.

A room with a working past

When the station closed in 1994, the room could have become just another guest bedroom. The Ansel family kept the old desk and the tall window instead. Today the room is a small library, open to anyone staying in the house.

The desk carries a few marks from its working years. The shelves hold sea charts, field guides, and novels left behind by guests. At certain times of day, the light crosses the floor exactly as it did when the room was still on watch.

Keep the view, change the use

Not everything old needs to be frozen in place. The house has changed, the coast has changed, and the room has a quieter job now. What stayed was the part worth keeping: a good window, a clear view, and enough time to look out.

The Watch Room library is open from morning until late. Find a book, take the window seat, and see what is passing the point.
